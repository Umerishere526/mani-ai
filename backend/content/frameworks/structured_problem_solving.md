Starts when: you have learned the one practical problem, that a decision or action could change it, and that they do not know what to do, rather than knowing and not starting.
Sounds like: "I do not know what to do", "I need to make a decision", "every choice has a downside", "I do not know where to start", too many things at once.
Skip when: they know what to do but cannot begin (behavioral_activation), one troubling thought (thought_reframe), a belief about an event (abcde), what they cannot change (act_choice_point), or about to act (dbt_stop).
Stages: problem (one problem, in their words) > facts (known, assumed, unknown) > control (the part they can influence) > outcome (what they want it to achieve) > options (two or more real ways) > compare (what matters about each) > select (their own choice) > first_action (one small step) > closing (how it sits now)
Ends when: they have chosen their own response and one first step; they need not feel certain, and nothing promises it will work.
Offer: mirror the problem in their words and that they want a way forward, then ask if they want to work through it together.
Never: decide for them, promise an outcome, give a medical, legal or financial conclusion in place of an expert, or help plan retaliation, deception or an unsafe confrontation.
Never: treat abuse, a threat or danger as a communication problem or an ordinary decision; their safety comes before any plan.
