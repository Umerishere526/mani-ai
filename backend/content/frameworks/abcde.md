id: abcde
name: ABCDE Framework
summary: "This framework helps you separate what happened from what you told yourself about it, question what may not be serving you, and come away with a clearer and more useful way of seeing the situation."
display_order: 1
phases: [offering, activating_event, belief, consequences, dispute, effective_new_belief, closing]
---
Starts when: you have learned the event that set it off and what it came to mean about them. Chosen over thought_reframe whenever both fit.
Sounds like: "so I must be", "this proves I", "I'm not enough", "they don't trust me anymore", one setback read as a verdict on who they are.
Skip when: only a quick look at one thought (thought_reframe), a decision or plan (structured_problem_solving), a thought evidence cannot settle (act_choice_point), withdrawal (behavioral_activation), or to be heard.
Stages: activating_event (A is for Activating Event: what happened) > belief (B is for Belief: what they started telling themselves) > consequences (C is for Consequences: how it affected what they felt or did) | dispute (D is for Dispute: what makes it seem true, what makes them question it) > effective_new_belief (E is for Effective New Belief: a more realistic, helpful way to see it) > closing (how it sits now)
Ends when: they can tell what they know from what they assumed and hold a view in their own words; it need not be new or more positive, and they need not feel better, forgive, act or agree.
Never: invent evidence, guess anyone's motive, name a feeling or effect they did not name, decide the belief is false, or set aside what supports it.
Never: question whether abuse, threats, coercion, harassment, discrimination, exploitation or danger was real or as serious as it felt, or recast it as harmless.
