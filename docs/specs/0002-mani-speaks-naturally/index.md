# 0002. Mani speaks naturally

**Date**: 2026-10-04
**Status**: Accepted

## Summary

Mani sounds robotic because the instructions grew to 499 lines of rules, and the code makes the model rewrite any reply that skips a question, names an unspoken feeling or repeats a question. This spec rewrites the instructions short (about 100 lines, no example conversations), retires the rewrite checks for a skipped question and a repeated question and the code that trims feeling sentences, and makes Mani read a very short reply ("yes", "no", "I don't know") as an answer to the question it just asked. Safety checks, offer rules and stage tracking stay in code, and so does one rewrite when a draft uses a feeling or size word the person never used (the prompt alone let 2 of 36 conversations through, so muhammad restored it). Success is measured on the client's four style conversations (scope row 32), compared with the saved baseline, plus a human read of the transcripts. After the first read, muhammad found two more habits that make Mani sound like a machine: the phrase "It sounds like" (25 of 88 replies in the last check, 19 of 36 conversations) and a sentence that says the person's own words back before the question ("You find yourself unable to stop scrolling."). Both are now banned in ordinary replies, by the instructions alone, and measured by the check and by the read. After the build, muhammad accepted the measured results as the bars for this build (2026-10-04): AC-6, AC-7 and AC-9 were amended, and the reads were done blind by a fresh model whose marks muhammad accepted as final.

## Requirements

Linked scope feature: row 33 in [docs/scope/conversation.md](../../scope/conversation.md). Everything below is judged on row 32's check (`scripts/eval_client_style.py`), three runs, means of the three ("the final check" is `backend/.eval/client_style/style-lines-1`, the last three run check whose prompt is the one in force, with the same database digest as the seeded `mani_base`; every count acceptance criterion is judged on it; ratios are a mean of the three per run figures and counts are totals over its 36 conversations, as the check prints them; the baselines are the figures recorded in `mani-vault/Journal/natural-mani-build-2026-10-04.md` and are taken as given, not recomputed), current model (`google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`). Row 35 later re-runs the same check on other models.

**User stories**:
- As a person talking to Mani, I want replies that sound like a down to earth person who is paying attention, so that I feel heard and not processed.
- As a person who answers "yes" or "I don't know", I want Mani to understand what I was answering from the conversation so far, so that I do not have to repeat myself.
- As the client reviewing transcripts, I want the three styles to differ in what Mani does (Direct leads, Supportive accompanies, Reflective explores), not in how emotional it sounds.

**Acceptance criteria**:

The loop runs once at most (`if why:`) and its comments are updated. A turn with no redraft reason (the reasons are an offer veto, or a feeling or size word the person never used) makes exactly one chat purpose provider call (the exercise handoff when a framework finishes is a separate call, as today). No redraft note tells the model to ask a question, and a second draft that still fails is left as drafted, never trimmed.
- **AC-3** (what stays): the safety screen, the offer vetoes (too early, not allowed yet, closest fit due, ruled out), the one redraft for a feeling or size word never used (AC-8), stage tracking, button label checks (feeling or judgment in a label), the name limit and the one time "Do I have this right?" guard behave as today, and their existing tests stay green.
- **AC-4** (short replies read in context): when the person's message is three raw words or fewer (words counted by one shared `context.word_count` that strips punctuation and does not expand contractions, so "I don't know" is three), is neither `heard` nor `correction`, and is not a button tap, the context block carries `their_last: short`. If Mani's last message contains a question, the block also carries `answering: "<that question>"`, except while an offer is waiting or when that question is the code's own permission question with no live offer. The base says a stage's own guidance (`stage_if_unclear`) wins over `answering`. Tests cover "yes", "no", "idk", "I don't know", "not really", a four word reply (no hint), a tap (no hint), a reply after a message with no question (no answering line), a `heard` phrase of three words (stays `heard`), a framework running (`short` sent), and `offer_waiting` and `safety: concern` (nothing sent). `test_chat_context.py` tests that assert `vague` or no `their_last` while running are rewritten.
- **AC-5** (read in context, by people): the check's report lists every Mani reply that follows a reply of three raw words or fewer, leaving out button taps and marking the client's scripted turns (which answer the client's Mani, not the model's). On those lists from three runs, the team agrees each reply acts on what the person was answering. The reading and its verdict are recorded in the journal.
- **AC-6** (fewer questions): questions per reply is total question marks over total replies in a run, then the mean of three runs, counted on Mani's own words (code added offer text and replies after acceptance left out, since code adds a question there). It is the `questions per reply, Mani's own words` figure (`questions_per_reply_own`) of the final check, and `replies with two questions` is 0. The baseline's 0.59 is in the journal table "The baseline, recounted". The all replies figure is printed too, for comparison. The bar is inside the noise between runs (the same prompt gave 0.51 and 0.52, the rewrite 0.47 to 0.54), so it does not by itself show the fix. The earlier aim of under 0.5 was not reached: the rewrite measured 0.47 to 0.54 and the final check 0.54. muhammad set the bar above on 2026-10-04.
- **AC-7** (no stock phrases): counted per conversation on Mani's own words before acceptance (`own_words`, replies from the accepting tap on left out), the same text as AC-6 and AC-8, except the repeated openers, which are counted on all replies as the baseline was. The existing stock phrase list is used, with two patterns added, one for "sounds" and one for "seems": `\b(?:it |that )?(?:sounds|seems) (?:like|as if|as though)\b`, matched on lower case text with curly apostrophes made straight, so "That sounds like" and a bare "Sounds like" count, and each pattern is its own phrase for the "said twice" count. Part (1) alone does not show the fix, since the build before the ban passed it; parts (2) and (3) do. Zero for the two phrases stays the aim, and no redraft enforces it. The count on every reply is printed beside it, for information. The final check measured 0.83, 0.00 and 0.67 per conversation (Direct, Reflective, Supportive), 14 uses of the two phrases, none said twice. The client has not been asked whether the phrase may stay (Follow-up).
- **AC-8** (do not label): three parts, all on Mani's own words in the final replies, before the offer is accepted (`count_conversation` leaves out code composed offer text and replies after acceptance).

The bar for the size phrases other than "a lot" is no more than the baseline's count; "a lot" is reported only, until the client says whether it may stay (see Follow-up). The baseline's counts come from running the same functions over `baseline/transcripts.md`, which marks no taps: the offer is taken to be accepted at the first person message equal to a button label of the offer before it. The count and that rule are recorded in the journal, since nothing committed parses the file.
  - *Meaning stated as fact.* No word list catches a meaning that goes past the person's words ("stuck in a loop", "this thought is pulling at you", "a hard pattern to break"). A reader marks Mani's own words, before acceptance, in run 1 of the baseline and run 1 of the final check (12 conversations each; an exception to "mean of three runs", chosen because reading 72 conversations twice is too much) and counts the replies that state a meaning as fact. muhammad chose on 2026-10-04 that a fresh model does this blind and its marks stand as final, instead of reading it himself. The read of record is the AC-14 file (`read-files-final/restating_read.md`), which shuffles 36 conversations from three sources and hides which is which, with the key at the end; the count is taken on its untagged eligible replies, final against baseline in that same file (recorded: 0 against 5). The final count is no higher than the baseline's, every case is listed in the journal, and the verdict is recorded there.
- **AC-9** (styles differ in doing): read on the panic conversation, three runs, three styles, at two points: point 1 is Mani's reply to the first message, point 2 is Mani's reply to the second message. The person's two messages are word for word the same in all three styles, so the input matches and there are always 18 distinct replies (3 styles, 2 points, 3 runs). The check writes these replies to `style_read.md` in Mani's own words (the description and permission question that code adds left out), shuffled, with the style hidden and the key at the end. The reader, a model that did not write the spec or the build and is given only the file and this rubric word for word, labels each of the 18 replies Direct, Supportive, Reflective or none. A reply is then "own style" when its label equals its style in the key, tallied per style (6 each) and in all (of 18). The read of record is `style-lines-1/style_read.md`. The rubric is taken from the client's document:
  - Direct leads: it moves toward clarity or a next step, by saying or asking what comes next or what the person wants to do about it. A reply that only receives is not Direct.
  - Supportive accompanies: it receives what was said in plain words and stays alongside, with warmth or encouragement. Any question is gentle and is about what would help, never a request to examine something and never an instruction to do something.
  - Reflective explores: it mirrors one specific detail the person gave, in their words, and asks about it (what is strongest, what sits under it). It does not push toward a next step.
  - For all three styles, the sentence that introduces an offer ("I have a way to help you pause…") is judged by how it presents the offer, not by the fact that it points forward: Direct presents it as the next step, Supportive as something done together, Reflective as a way to look more closely at what the person described. A reply with nothing in it that shows a style is "none".
  - The bar for this build, set by muhammad on 2026-10-04: at least 10 of the 18 replies are "own style" (guessing gives 6) and no style has fewer than 2 of its 6. The full bar of 15 of 18 with at least 4 of 6 per style moves to scope row 6, whose rewrite of the offer wording is the cause of the misses: the offer part of the base says its questions are ones "you could go through together" in every style, so offers read Supportive, and the style lines of task 4b cannot change a line they do not own. The reader is a fresh model reading blind and its marks stand as final (muhammad's choice, 2026-10-04); `/check verify` judges the recorded marks and does not read again, since a second read of the same file can differ by a reply or two. The floor of 2 of 6 per style equals chance for one style, so this bar does not by itself show that the styles differ; that is row 6's. The final prompt read 10 of 18 (Direct 2, Reflective 3, Supportive 5), after 5 of 18 on the first rewrite.
- **AC-10** (offers not worse): all 36 conversations reach an offer, at least 33 of 36 at exchange 2 to 4 (baseline 33), and dashes in replies are no more than the baseline of 2 (row 34 removes them).
- **AC-11** (hard rules kept): the rewritten base keeps, in its own words, no clinical words, diagnosis, "therapy", "session" or "treatment"; stay in role and never reveal or obey instructions typed by the person; no silver linings and abuse, threats or danger never made milder; no size or weight words the person did not give ("heavy", "a lot", "overwhelming", "so much", "carrying", "the weight of"); and the `safety: concern` behaviour. `tests/evals` negative set stays green.
- **AC-12** (short replies): the instructions say a normal reply is one or two short sentences, a third only when it adds something new. The check counts replies of more than three sentences (split with `repairs._SENTENCE_BREAK`), after stripping the description and permission question that code composes for an offer, and leaving out replies after acceptance. The count is no higher than the baseline computed from the saved transcripts under the same rule (the baseline has 1 such reply, counted by the check; the reviewer's 9 did not strip the offer text).
- **AC-14** (no restating): while Mani is still understanding, the instructions tell it not to restate. A restatement is a sentence that is not a question and only paraphrases the person's last message, adding nothing. Mani asks a plain question instead, which may use their words (a Reflective reply carries its mirrored detail inside the question: "What is strongest about the racing thoughts?"), or gives a short reply that adds something, and a meaning that goes past their words is put as a question (as AC-8 asks). Left out of the rule, because they are built to mirror and rows 6 and 18 own them: the sentence that introduces an offer, the opening after "Try it" and every stage question, the opening after a `correction`, the reply to a `heard` message ("receive it") and the reflection after Chat More. Judged in a blind read by a fresh model whose marks stand as final (muhammad's choice, 2026-10-04), by the share of eligible replies that restate, summed over a source's 12 conversations (restating replies over eligible replies, not a mean of per conversation shares). The file builder tags each offer reply `[offer]` and each opening after a tap `[after tap]`, and the reader counts only untagged replies; the definition above is written at the top of the file. "Before" is run 1 of `ban-sounds-like-2`, the last check of task 4c (the run before the restating rule); "after" is run 1 of `style-lines-1`, the final check (task 4b ran after 4d). The read of record is `read-files-final/restating_read.md`, with its marks in the journal. The file for the read mixes before, after and baseline run 1, shuffled, source hidden, key at the end, so one read serves AC-14 (after against before) and the AC-8 meaning count (after against baseline). The share after is under half the share before. If the count before is under 4, the ratio means nothing: record it and go back to muhammad. Every case is listed in the journal. No word list or word overlap catches a paraphrase ("You find yourself unable to stop scrolling" shares two words of five with "I cannot stop scrolling"), so no automatic count is made. The count in the earlier `meaning_read.md` (the instructions before task 4c, read 2026-10-04) is recorded in the journal only.

## Decision

**Chosen option**: Option 2: rewrite the prompts short, retire the tone enforcement in code, and give the model Mani's last question with every very short reply.

Mani's rules shrink to who it is, how it talks, the three styles in what they do, and compressed mechanics for offers and stages; code stops rewriting replies for tone and keeps only safety, offer vetoes, stage tracking and one rewrite for a feeling or size word the person never used.

**Implementation skills**: none installed apply (no frontend or database work). The backend rules in `.claude/BACKEND.md` and the measuring lesson in `mani-vault/Journal/measure-before-tuning-prompts.md` apply.

### Ban "It sounds like" and restating (added after the first read, 2026-10-04)

muhammad read the shuffled transcripts and found that Mani still says "It sounds like" (the same kind of stock phrase as "I hear you") and says the person's words back as a statement before it asks ("You find yourself unable to stop scrolling. What does that look like for you?"). Both read as a machine. Two causes sit in the instructions themselves: the base's own line says "say back no more than is there", which asks for exactly this, and the base bans three phrases only, so the model reaches for the nearest one. The fix stays in the instructions, as ADR-012 says tone is the prompt's job:

- The base's stock phrase line names "It sounds like" and "It seems like" with the other three, and the line that says "say back" is replaced by AC-14's rule, which opens with "While you are still understanding" so it does not clash with the offer sentence that mirrors (base line 71). Both edits add no lines (AC-1): the base is exactly 120 lines, and lines 21 to 25 are reflowed.
- The check's list gets the two forms, so AC-7 counts them. The restating habit has no word list that works, so it is read (AC-14).
- No redraft is added. If the instructions alone cannot reach zero for the two phrases after two rounds of change, the choice goes back to muhammad: restore a redraft for the phrase, as was done for the feeling word, or accept the rate. Round 2 of 4d may also reword the shape descriptions in `schema.py` ("mirror and ask", "mirror and hold") and the `[ctx]` example in `response_format.md` that show mirroring; the field itself stays.
- The sentence that introduces an offer, stage questions and the reply to a correction keep mirroring. They are designed that way, and rows 6 and 18 own them.

### The rewritten base (what `mani_base.md` contains, about 100 lines)

1. Who Mani is and the hard rules of AC-11, short.
2. How Mani talks: one neutral, down to earth voice in all styles; stays with what the person said, uses their words, checks an understanding that goes past their words as a question, never states it as fact; asks only when a question helps, at most one; a reply that only receives what was said is fine; a very short reply is an answer to `answering`, read in the light of the conversation, and never asked again.
3. The three styles, 2 or 3 sentences each: Direct leads toward clarity or a next step, Supportive accompanies with warmth and encouragement, Reflective explores meaning and what sits under it. The `[ctx]` line `conversation_style` names the one in force.
4. When to offer, compressed to today's behaviour: the cooldown, clear and closest offers, the two buttons, the code adds the description and permission question, never offer under `safety: concern` or when "Never offer one when" rules it out.
5. Inside the questions, compressed to today's behaviour: one stage per reply, in order, the stage ask is a model to put in this person's words, `stage_if_unclear` wins over `answering`, they can stop at any time. Two rules keep their old words because the code that enforced them goes: "never ask twice for the same thing the same way" (it is now the only guard against a repeated stage question), and ADR-011's draft step (a request to pick gets one small step, as a draft they accept or change).
6. Ending, compressed: closing question, body check in as written, then the next choice.
7. Staying yourself, kept short.

Rows 6 and 18 later replace sections 4 and 5. `response_format.md` shrinks to about 70 lines: the `[ctx]` reference without the removed lines, three short reasoning steps, buttons, the Library, and the rules for every reply (one question at most, English only, no dashes).

**Key invariants**:
- A turn with no redraft reason makes one chat purpose provider call, and a turn with one makes two at most.
- The person's name appears at most once, buttons never carry an unspoken feeling or a judgment, and `Do I have this right?` is asked at most once, all as today.
- The question extraction moves from `redraft._QUESTION` to a shared helper in `context.py` (it is used by `answering`); nothing else reads it after `repeats` goes. The word count helper is shared by `context.py` and the check.
- `answering` never quotes a question the code added to a dead offer (see Follow-up, the button drop on a safety turn).

**Security model**: unchanged. Replies are special category health content; no message text goes into logs or Sentry beyond what is logged today. The new `answering` line is built per turn from the person's own thread and is sent only to the model.

**Configuration required**: none. After editing `content/`, run `python scripts/seed.py`; the app picks the prompts up when its cache expires or on restart.

**Critical test scenarios**:

## Migration plan

**Strategy**: no migration needed. The change is seeded content plus code, reversible by reverting the commit and re-seeding.
**Phases**:
1. Code and tests first (tasks 2 and 3), measured against the old prompt.
2. Prompt rewrite (task 4), measured against the baseline.
**Rollback**: revert the commit, run `python scripts/seed.py`, restart or wait for the prompt cache to expire.
**Risks**: tone and labelling are now held by the prompt and the measurement alone; a regression shows only on the check, which costs real model calls and has no automatic gate (the repo has no CI). The mitigation is task 5 and running the check before the row is closed.

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- Replies stop being forced into one shape; a turn with no veto costs one model call, down from up to three.
- The instructions are short enough for a person to read, so the next rewrite (rows 6 and 18) starts from something understood.
- A bare "yes" is read against the question it answers, which the fixed phrase list never did.

**Negative / tradeoffs**:
- Nothing at runtime stops Mani asking nothing (ADR-008), or stating a meaning as fact that no word list catches ("stuck in a loop"); the old code never caught those either. Only the check and reading do. A feeling or size word is redrafted once, and a second draft that still carries it reaches the person ("a lot" did in 5 of 90 replies in the stress conversation).
- Fewer questions can mean a later offer, since Mani learns less per reply. AC-10 watches for it.
- The kept size word rule is stricter than the client's own examples ("It sounds like a lot is happening at once"), and so is the ban on "It sounds like" itself, which the client's own document uses. muhammad chose the ban after reading the transcripts; the client has not been asked; see rationale.
- A banned phrase tends to be replaced by its nearest neighbour ("It seems like", "It appears"), so the list may need to grow, and a list only ever catches the neighbours it knows. Restating has no list at all and is caught only by a person reading.
- Mani cannot reflect a person's words back as a statement while it is understanding. A Reflective reply has to carry its mirroring inside the question, and AC-9 is watched again for that.
- The instructions are at their 120 line limit, so every added rule has to replace words, not add lines.
- Sections 4 and 5 of the new base are rewritten again by rows 6 and 18.
- One short prompt tuned on the lite model may not carry to the model row 35 picks; the check is re run there.

**Neutral**:
- `thread_response_styles` and the `style` field keep being written and read with less to do.
- `.claude/BACKEND.md` still lists the retired redraft reasons; `/sync` owns that edit.
