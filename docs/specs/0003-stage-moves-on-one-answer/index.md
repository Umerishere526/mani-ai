# 0003. A framework stage moves on after one answer

**Date**: 2026-10-04
**Status**: In Progress

## Summary

Inside a framework (a set of guided questions, such as ABCDE or Behavioral Activation), Mani now asks each stage's question once and moves to the next stage after any reply: a real answer, "I don't know", "yes", "sure", or a question of the person's own. Today the model decides when a stage is done and often asks the same thing again in other words. That is the robotic, quizzing feel the client objected to. The code now moves the stage forward before the model is called. The model only sees the next stage's question, so it has nothing old to repeat. Stages whose question depends on an earlier answer get a second, authored question to use when that answer never came. After the first real runs, two bounded holds were added: a person who does not understand a question gets it said again once in simpler words, and a person who cannot choose between options they already named is offered one of them with a reason. A stage can take at most one extra turn, counted in the database, so a conversation cannot loop on its own (a safety redirect is the one exception). The first measurement on the real model showed the model marks a redirect wrongly every time, so the model no longer marks one: the code decides from the reply, and a hold is a redirect only when the reply (or Mani's message just before it) carries the words of a safety, fit or protective branch or one of the client's lines. A stage that picks among options gets a note of its own that leads with the offer, so a request to suggest something is held, not folded into the next question. A person who says they did not understand a question is detected in code: on that turn the model is shown only the answered stage's own question, so it can only say it again, and the code records the hold itself. The real model held on fewer than half of those requests when it was left to decide.

## Requirements

**User stories**:
- As a person going through a framework, I want Mani to take whatever I say, even "I don't know", and go on, so that I am not quizzed for an answer I do not have.
- As a person who asks Mani something in the middle of a framework, I want my question answered and then the next step, so that the conversation still moves.
- As a person who asks Mani to choose for me, I want one small suggestion built from what I said, so that I am not handed a list when I have said I cannot choose.
- As a person who does not understand a question, I want Mani to say it again in simpler words once, so that I can answer it, and to move on if I still cannot.
- As a person who cannot choose between options I named, I want Mani to suggest one with a reason and ask if it suits me, so that I am helped without being decided for.
- As the client, I want the framework to still reach the body check in, so that every set of questions ends the way the style document describes.

**Acceptance criteria**:
A **move on turn** is a turn where a framework runs (`running` in `context.build`: no `safety: concern`), the offer was not accepted on this turn (`framework_starting` false), and the stored stage is after `offering` and before `somatic_checkin`. Every other turn (no framework, a waiting offer, a typed or tapped yes, a stored `offering` for any reason, a stored somatic stage, a safety pause) keeps today's `[ctx]` and today's transition rules.

- **AC-1** (code moves the stage on): on a move on turn, `context.build` puts the stage after the stored one in `[ctx]` as `stage` with its `purpose`, `listen_for`, `boundaries`, `if_earlier_missing` and `ask`. The stored stage appears only as `answered`, with its `purpose` and those of its `if_unclear` branches not marked `start_only`; its `ask` is never sent. Neither `stage_ready_when` nor any `next_stage_*` line is sent, and `current_phase` names the answered stage. When the stage to ask is `somatic_checkin` (stored `closing`), it is sent in full as today, with its `if_unclear` and `ready_when`. A unit test asserts each of these. The one exception is a request to hear the question again (AC-17), where the stage after the stored one is not sent and the stored stage's own `ask` is sent as `answered_ask`.
- **AC-2** (what gets recorded): on a move on turn the stage recorded is the stored stage only when the reply reports it, which is a hold (AC-4, AC-12). In every other case the stage after the stored one is recorded: the reply reports it, reports one further ahead, reports an earlier stage, reports a stage the framework does not have, names another framework, or leaves `state` out. A correction keeps today's `corrected phase` note, and an omitted state notes `no state, recorded <stage>`. The orchestrator writes the stage in every one of these cases (today it writes nothing without a state), so a lost or garbled state can never keep a stage on screen. On every other turn `validate_transition` and `clamp` behave as today. Unit tests cover the hold, the move, the skip, the step back, the unknown stage, the omitted state, and a stored somatic stage stepping as today; the tests that expect a step back to record the stored stage, and an unknown stage to drop the framework state, change.
- **AC-3** (start turns are unchanged): on the turn they accept by tapping, `[ctx]` still shows the first stage in full (with its `ready_when` and all its branches) and the second stage's question, and the recorded step may be the first or the second stage (ADR 011). A typed yes (accepted after the call, `deferred` in `orchestrator`) and a stored `offering` after a tapped start with no state also keep today's `[ctx]` and rules. The existing start turn tests in `test_chat_context.py` and `test_turn.py` stay green.
- **AC-4** (the holds): a reply may stay on the answered stage, reporting it as `state.step`, in these cases only. *Counted* holds, at most one per stage (AC-12): the person says they did not understand the question or asks for it another way (AC-14); the person cannot choose between options they already named, at a stage marked `picks_from_options` (AC-13); and, in DBT STOP's `stop` and `pause` stages, they are acting on the urge right now (the five branches there carry `counted: true` in the file, and `[ctx]` shows each to the model as using its extra turn). *Redirect* holds are not counted, and the code, not the model, decides which holds they are (AC-15): a hold is a redirect when the reply, or the Mani message before it, carries the words of a branch of the answered stage that is neither `counted` nor `start_only` (safety, a framework that does not fit, the protective step), or one of the client's three lines (another issue comes in, "I misunderstood what you meant", they want to stop). Every other hold is counted. After a hold the next turn shows the same `stage` again, because it was shown but not asked. The answered stage is asked again only by the one rephrase of AC-14. `safety: concern` keeps pausing the framework as today.
- **AC-5** (stuck, and asking Mani to choose): "I don't know", "not sure", "idk", "suggest something", "you pick" and "tell me what to do" all go on to the next stage at once, with no options and no draft, except at a stage marked `picks_from_options` when they have options on the table, which is AC-13. At any other stage Mani says plainly that this one is theirs to say and goes on. Any other question of their own is answered first, then the next stage is asked in the same reply. These are base prompt rules, measured in AC-9.
- **AC-6** (content): in all six framework files, `ready_when` remains only on the first stage after `offering` (no `offering` stage has one). Every `if_unclear` branch marked *drop* in the inventory (`rationale.md`) is removed; branches marked *keep* stay. The first stage keeps all of its branches, and those that ask the same stage again (marked *start only* in the inventory) carry `start_only: true`. The wording is not scripted. muhammad reads every such reply in AC-9 for plain, natural, down to earth English that leaves the person feeling heard. ## Decision

**Chosen option**: Option 1: code moves the stage on before the model call, and authored content handles what a later stage asks when an earlier answer is missing.

The stored stage is the stage whose question Mani last asked. On the person's next message the code shows only the stage after it, limits what the reply may record to that stage or a hold, and removes from content everything that told the model to wait (`ready_when` after the first stage, and the branches that ask the same stage again).

**Bounded holds (added 2026-10-04, after the first real runs).** muhammad's two holds are one rule: a stage may take at most one extra turn, counted in `thread_technique_state.holds`. The counted reasons are saying a question again once (AC-14), offering one option at a `picks_from_options` stage (AC-13), and DBT STOP's acting hold. Redirects (safety, the client's lines) are not counted, because a counter must never move a person who has described abuse on to the next stage. The code decides which holds are redirects, from the reply's words (AC-15); every other hold counts, so a missed redirect limits the conversation instead of letting it loop. The code enforces the limit by recording the next stage, and `[ctx]` tells the model when the extra turn is used.

**The redirect is decided in code, from the reply (added 2026-10-04, after the 54 real conversations).** In those runs the model set `state.redirect` on 24 holds, and none was a redirect: nine were DBT STOP `pause`, whose branches are counted, eleven were at stages that have no redirect branch at all, and six were rephrases at stages that do. Because a redirect is not counted, the limit under read and two stages took two holds. The model no longer marks anything. A hold is a redirect when the reply, or Mani's message just before it, carries the words of a redirect branch of the answered stage or one of the client's three lines, and a reply that carries them is recorded as a redirect hold even when it reported the next stage (AC-15). The cost is that a real redirect the model paraphrases is counted, and a second hold at that stage then records the next stage. The previous message rule buys one more turn for the follow up to a safety reply, the deterministic safety screen is the backstop for explicit risk, and ending or pausing the framework on a safety branch is recorded as follow up work. Separately, a stage that picks among options gets a `stage_note` of its own that leads with the offer, because the shared note put the exception after "ask stage_ask now" and said "cannot choose", so "suggest something" was folded into the next question and the model chose for the person (AC-16). A request to hear a question again is handled in code instead (added 2026-10-04, after verify, AC-17). The first measurement held 25 of 54, and the shared note again put the exception after "ask stage_ask now". The code recognises the request from a short list of phrases and an eight word cap, and on that turn shows the model only the answered stage's own question (`answered_ask`) with a note that tells it to say that question again in simpler words, so there is no next question to fold the request into. The code records the counted hold itself, whatever the model reports. A request in other words falls to the model, as before, and a second request after the extra turn is used moves on.

**Implementation skills**: none installed apply (no frontend or database work). The backend rules in `.claude/BACKEND.md`, and the lessons in `mani-vault/Journal/panic-and-already-answered-stages.md` and `framework-files-what-the-model-actually-reads.md`, apply.

## Feature design

**Data model sketch**: one new column, `thread_technique_state.holds smallint not null default 0 check (holds between 0 and 1)`, by migration `011_technique_state_holds.sql`, with no new grant. `holds` is the number of counted holds on the stored stage, and goes back to 0 on every move. `thread_technique_state.phase` keeps its meaning: the stage whose question Mani last asked. The framework content (seeded into `admin.frameworks.stages`, a JSON column the seed passes through as it is) changes shape per stage:

| Stage field | First stage after `offering` | Later framework stages |
|---|---|---|
| `purpose`, `listen_for`, `boundaries`, `ask` | as today | as today |
| `ready_when` | kept | removed |
| `if_unclear` | all branches kept; those marked *start only* in the inventory gain `start_only: true` | only branches marked *keep* in the inventory |
| `if_earlier_missing` | never | on the 27 listed stages: `{needs: <earlier stage id>, reply: <text, or a map with all three styles>}` |
| `picks_from_options` | never | `true` on Behavioral Activation `choose` and Structured Problem Solving `select` only |

The `offering` stage and the two somatic stages are not changed.

New phase lists: ABCDE `offering, activate, belief, consequence, evidence_for, evidence_against, balanced, closing`; Thought Reframe `offering, thought, significance, facts_for, facts_against, alternative, reframe, closing`; each followed by the two somatic stages the seed appends. The first half keeps the old stage's `ask`. The second half's `ask`, scenario free so that `test_negative_set.py` passes:

| Stage | supportive | reflective | direct |
|---|---|---|---|
| ABCDE `evidence_against` | What might challenge that belief? | What might challenge that belief? | What might challenge that belief? |
| Thought Reframe `facts_against` | What else do you know about the situation? | What does not support the full conclusion? | What facts do not fit that conclusion? |

The `purpose`, `listen_for` and `boundaries` of the two halves are split from the old stage's text, supporting half first. The old stage's branch that held its second question is removed (it became the second half's `ask`).

**State transitions** (a running framework, stored stage `P`):
- Stored `offering` (the offer accepted by tap or typed yes, or a tapped start with no state): today's rules, so `offering` → first stage or second stage (ADR 011).
- Move on turn (`P` after `offering`, before `somatic_checkin`): `P` → stage after `P` (the move), or `P` → `P` (an AC-4 hold). Nothing else is recorded.
A reported hold that is not a redirect (the reply and the Mani message before it carry no redirect evidence), with `holds` 0: `P` → `P`, `holds` 0 → 1. A second one, with `holds` 1: `P` → stage after `P`, `holds` back to 0, note `hold limit at P`. A redirect hold, including a reply that carries the evidence but reported the next stage: `P` → `P`, `holds` unchanged, 0 or 1. Anything else on a move on turn, including an omitted state: `P` → stage after `P`, `holds` 0. Every other turn that records a stage writes `holds` 0; a turn that records nothing leaves it.
- `closing` → `somatic_checkin` is an ordinary move; from a stored `somatic_checkin` on, the body route and today's rules own the turns.
- `safety: concern`: paused as today, with no stage in `[ctx]`. When it clears, the stored stage is unchanged and the next turn is a move on turn from it.
- A stored stage that is not in the phase list (after the split, a local thread at `examine` or `facts`): today's behaviour, which falls back to the `offering` stage.

**Interface surface**: the structured reply schema (`Reply`) is unchanged. The `[ctx]` block on a move on turn:

```
current_phase: <stored stage id>
active_framework: <id>
framework_stages: <every stage id, in order>
answered: <stored stage id>
answered_purpose: <its purpose>
answered_picks_options: yes                                                             (only when the answered stage has picks_from_options and holds is 0)
hold_used: yes                                                                   (only when holds is 1)
asked_again: yes                                                                 (only on a rephrase turn, AC-17: the stage block below is then replaced by answered_ask)
answered_if_unclear: <its branches without start_only, resolved to the style; a counted branch reads 'if X (uses your extra turn): reply'>   (omitted when none)
stage: <the stage after it>
stage_purpose / stage_listen_for / stage_boundaries
stage_if_earlier_missing: if nothing usable was said at <needs>: <reply>        (only on the 27)
stage_if_unclear / stage_ready_when                                              (only when stage is a somatic stage)
stage_ask: <resolved to the style>
stage_note: <below>
```

The `stage_note` on a move on turn when `holds` is 0 and `answered_picks_options` is not sent, word for word:

> stage_note: they have replied to the answered stage, so it is done. Ask stage_ask now, in their words, never bare. If stage_if_earlier_missing is given and nothing usable was said at that stage, ask that instead. Report stage as your step. Stay on the answered stage, reporting answered as your step, only in these cases: they did not understand the question, so say it again once in simpler everyday words; or a branch in answered_if_unclear applies (a branch marked uses your extra turn uses it up) or you use one of the client's lines, so use its reply as written

When `answered_picks_options` is sent (the stage picks among options and `holds` is 0):

> stage_note: they have replied to the answered stage, which asks them to choose among options they named. If they named two or more options in this conversation and say they do not know, cannot choose, or ask you to suggest or pick one, stay on the answered stage, reporting answered as your step, and do not ask stage_ask: offer ONE of their options with a short reason from what they said, and ask whether it suits them or another would be easier. Never decide for them. Otherwise it is done: if they asked you to choose, say plainly that this one is theirs to say, then ask stage_ask now, in their words, never bare. If stage_if_earlier_missing is given and nothing usable was said at that stage, ask that instead. Report stage as your step. Also stay on the answered stage only if they did not understand the question, so say it again once in simpler everyday words, or a branch in answered_if_unclear applies or you use one of the client's lines, so use its reply as written

When `asked_again` is sent (a rephrase turn, which takes the place of either note above):

> stage_note: they say they did not understand your last message or ask for it another way. Stay on the answered stage, reporting answered as your step, and ask no other question: say again the question your last message asked, in simpler, shorter, everyday words, as ONE question, using answered_ask as its model, with nothing added after it, no new topic, no new example and no explaining of the method. If your last message asked none or more than one, say answered_ask again in simpler words instead. If a branch in answered_if_unclear applies, use its reply as written instead, with nothing added

When `holds` is 1:

> stage_note: you have already stayed on the answered stage once, so it is done whatever they said. Ask stage_ask now, in their words, never bare. If stage_if_earlier_missing is given and nothing usable was said at that stage, ask that instead. Report stage as your step. If your last message offered one of their options, they are answering that, so use the one they chose in what you ask. If they say they still did not understand, say in a clause that the next question may help, then ask stage_ask. Only a branch in answered_if_unclear that is not marked uses your extra turn, or one of the client's lines, may keep you on the answered stage, and then use its reply as written

On a rephrase turn (`asked_again: yes`) the block is: `current_phase`, `active_framework`, `framework_stages`, `answered`, `answered_purpose`, `answered_ask: <the stored stage's ask, resolved to the style>`, `asked_again: yes`, `answered_if_unclear` (when there is one), `stage_note` (the rephrase note), and no `stage` line, no `answered_picks_options`, no `hold_used`.

Every other turn's block is unchanged. `answering` and `their_last: short` from spec 0002 are still sent; the base says the answered stage's branches come before `answering`.

**Key invariants**:
- A stage question Mani has asked is not in `[ctx]` again. After a hold, the stage shown but not asked is shown again; the answered stage is not. The one rephrase (AC-14) is written from the history.
- On a move on turn the recorded stage never moves backwards and never moves more than one stage.
- A stage takes at most two replies from the person, whatever the model reports: only an explicit hold keeps the stage, and it is counted unless the reply carries redirect evidence. So a framework that keeps receiving replies reaches `somatic_checkin` in at most two turns per stage plus the redirects. Redirects are not counted and are the one way a stage can take longer.
- `holds` is 0 or 1 and goes back to 0 on every move.
- A redirect hold is recorded only when the reply, or for a reported hold the Mani message before it, carries a redirect branch's words or one of the client's lines. Nothing the model reports exempts a hold from the count.
- `mani_base.md` stays at 120 lines or fewer.

**Security model**: the new column is a count with no message text. `authenticated` can read it with the rest of the row and cannot write it (insert and update stay with `mani_service`). The `held at <stage>` and `hold limit at <stage>` notes carry a stage id only, never message text. Replies remain special category health content and nothing new reaches logs or Sentry.

**Configuration required**: apply migration `011_technique_state_holds.sql` (`supabase db reset` locally), then run `python scripts/seed.py` after editing `content/`.

## Migration plan

**Strategy**: one additive migration (a column with a default, so existing rows read 0), then seeded content and code.
**Phases**:
1. Code and prompt (tasks 1 and 2) can land before the content, since a stage with no `if_earlier_missing` simply sends none.
2. Content per framework (tasks 3 and 4), seeded each time.
3. The migration first, then the hold code, prompt and content (tasks 6 and 7); a row written before the migration reads `holds` 0.
**Rollback**: revert the commit, run `python scripts/seed.py`, restart or wait for the prompt cache to expire. Migrations are forward only: leave the column, or drop it in a later migration.
**Risks**: a local thread stored at `examine` (ABCDE) or `facts` (Thought Reframe) when the split is seeded falls back to the `offering` stage on its next turn, because those ids no longer exist and today's transition check treats an unknown stored stage as nothing started. No real users run on this backend yet (the frontends still use placeholder data), so this is accepted rather than migrated; `supabase db reset` clears such threads locally.

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- The repeated stage question, the main "quizzing" complaint, becomes structurally hard: the old question is not in `[ctx]` and the record cannot go back.
- Every framework reaches the body check in a predictable number of turns.
- About fifty content branches and every `ready_when` after the first stage go, so the model reads less and nothing it reads tells it to wait.

**Negative / tradeoffs**:
- Later stages work with less. A person who says "I don't know" at ABCDE's belief step reaches the evidence steps with no belief named, and the authored `if_earlier_missing` question is all that carries the framework. The clinical depth of the client's framework is reduced, and the client has not yet confirmed this trade (open question in `docs/scope/index.md`).
- The branches that caught a wrong kind of answer (a feeling instead of a belief, an assumed motive, a step too large) are gone after the first stage. Mani no longer corrects those; it moves on.
- DBT STOP's `proceed` stage loses "still wants the original action". Someone who still wants to send the message goes on to the closing question rather than being asked what the message is for. Only `stop` and `pause` have counted holds; redirect branches elsewhere are not counted. A person still acting on the urge after one extra turn is moved to `observe`, whose question works either way.
- A model that reports the answered stage while actually asking the next one causes the next stage to be asked twice. Nothing in code catches this; AC-9's `repeated_question` count is the guard.
- 27 new questions to write and keep in step with the client's specifications, and the content departs further from `backend/docs/specs/`, which still describe `ready_when` and the branches.
- The holds are the model's judgement, and a redirect is recognised by its words. The client's three lines are in the base word for word. A branch's reply reaches the model only through `answered_if_unclear`, where the note tells it to use the reply as written, so how often it does is measured, not assumed. A real redirect the model paraphrases is counted, so a second hold records the next stage. The previous message rule means a person who keeps describing abuse after a quoted safety reply gets one more uncounted turn, and then the next stage is asked. Abuse described at a stage after the first has no branch to quote, and the safety screen reads only the current message for about ten explicit phrases, so it is a weaker backstop than it sounds.
- A model that ignores `hold_used` and holds again has its next stage recorded without that stage having been asked, so one stage's question is skipped. The framework still ends. `if_earlier_missing` covers the missing answer later, and the `hold limit` note shows how often it happens.
- A person who still does not understand a rephrased question is moved on after one try. That is deliberate, and the cost is that they may answer the next question without having answered this one.
- Redirects are not counted, so they are the one way a stage can take longer. They end in the person leaving the framework, not in a loop, but nothing bounds them in code.
- One more column, one more field on the reply's state, and two more `[ctx]` lines for the model to follow.
- After a redirect hold the last Mani message is the redirect, not the stage question, so a rephrase that follows it may say the wrong thing again. Rare, and accepted.
- A reply with no state or a garbled one records the next stage, so a lost state can never keep a stage on screen. The cost is the same as the forced move: if the model had in fact stayed, one question is not asked.
- The phrase list is short and the cap is eight words. A request in other words ("huh", "that is not clear", "explain") is not recognised and falls to the model. A statement about the situation with none of the blocked words ("I don't understand my own anger" is blocked by "my", "I don't understand people" is not) is recognised and costs one extra turn, which is bounded. A person who used the extra turn on a rephrase cannot get the pick offer at the same stage. A frustrated person who says they do not understand a second time gets a clause and the next question, not a third rephrase. If frustration shows up in the transcripts, the "stop here" line should be considered.

**Neutral**:
- `answered` and `stage` replace `stage` and `next_stage` in `[ctx]` on a move on turn, so the model reads two block shapes (move on turns, and every other turn), and `response_format.md` describes both.
- ADR-010 and ADR-011 are amended, not replaced; spec 0002's "stage validation and `ready_when` in `[ctx]`: Keep" row is narrowed to the start turn.
