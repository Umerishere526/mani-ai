## What it is

A Directive test where the user is torn between loyalty to her sister and feeling taken advantage of over
unpaid loans. She lists twelve failures, better responses, a table comparing both Directive tests, and a
bottom line. It states the motto: "We at mani do not fix a problem we discuss the challenge." muhammad marked
this message and the [speak up at work review](2026-10-18-directive-speak-up-at-work-review.md) as core: they
define what MANI is.

## The message

```
Muhammad and Manasa 

I am testing our chat and mani directive conversational chat style and I have run into many issues and concerns.

MANI is confusing being directive with moving the user toward a solution. We at mani do not fix a problem we discuss the challenge. That is our motto. 

It is not demonstrating the emotional understanding necessary to help someone work through a difficult decision.

The user comes into this conversation carrying guilt, a sense of family obligation, resentment, and an internal conflict between loving her sister and feeling taken advantage of.

MANI hears the financial problem but never meaningfully engages with the emotional conflict.

The 12 failures in this conversation
1. MANI identifies the circumstances but misses the core issue
The user says:

"My sister is going through a difficult time financially ... I love her ... I'm starting to feel taken advantage of. I feel guilty even thinking about saying no."

This statement contains almost everything MANI needs to recognize the emotional conflict.

The problem is not simply unpaid loans.

The deeper issue is that the user feels torn between her loyalty to family and her own needs, and experiences guilt at the thought of choosing herself.

MANI never adequately explores that conflict.

2. MANI ignores the strongest emotional signal
The user explicitly says:

"I feel guilty even thinking about saying no."

That is the most important emotional disclosure in the opening statement.

Yet MANI responds:

"So the requests keep coming, even though the money you've lent hasn't come back. What usually happens when you lend her money?"

MANI chooses the transactional detail and bypasses the emotional disclosure.

A more emotionally intelligent response would be:

"You love your sister and want to be there for her, but you're also feeling taken advantage of. And even considering saying no brings up guilt. What is it about saying no to your sister that feels so difficult?"

This is still direct. It simply addresses what matters.

3. MANI treats the situation as a financial problem rather than a relationship conflict
MANI repeatedly returns to the money, the loans, and the lack of repayment.

But the user is struggling with what saying no would mean about her as a sister.

MANI fails to recognize that the money is the circumstance, not necessarily the core issue.

4. MANI fails to explore the user's deeply held belief about family
The user says:

"She is my sister and family should come first before money."

This is a defining moment.

The user is revealing a deeply held value about loyalty, responsibility, and what it means to be family.

MANI could explore how that belief influences the user's feelings and choices.

Instead, it asks:

"What do you feel you can decide about lending her money?"

That question is disconnected from the emotional significance of what the user just shared.

5. MANI asks the user to answer the very question they came for help with
The user says:

"I do not know that is why I am discussing it with you."

This is a direct indication that MANI is failing to provide value.

The user came to gain clarity, yet MANI keeps returning the unresolved decision to them without helping them understand what makes it difficult.

A good conversation helps the user think. It does not simply keep asking the user to think harder.

6. MANI repeatedly asks variations of the same question:  Repetition 
MANI asks:

"What makes it hard to say no?"

"What do you feel you can decide?"

"What feels hardest to decide?"

"Have you told your sister you can't lend her any more?"

These questions differ in wording, but they repeatedly circle the same issue without developing the conversation.

The user has already explained that saying no conflicts with her beliefs about family.

MANI should move deeper into that conflict rather than continue asking about the decision itself.

7. MANI fails to recognize possible shame beneath the guilt
The user expresses guilt explicitly. Shame is also a possible underlying emotion, although the conversation does not establish it.

There is an important distinction.

Guilt might sound like: "I would feel bad refusing to help my sister."

Shame might sound like: "What kind of sister would I be if I refused to help her?"

MANI could gently explore this distinction without imposing either feeling on the user.

Instead, it never explores what the guilt means to the person experiencing it.

8. MANI introduces the ABCDE framework without understanding the person
MANI says:

"There's a framework called ABCDE that helps you identify the thoughts behind your guilt, question whether they're true, and replace them with more realistic ones."

This is a major failure.

The user has expressed a meaningful personal value about family.

MANI immediately offers to challenge the thoughts behind that value.

This risks communicating that the user's belief is irrational or needs correcting.

MANI has not established that the belief is inaccurate. It has not even explored what it means to the user.

The app is attempting an intervention before establishing understanding.

9. MANI does not recognize when the user feels misunderstood
The user says:

"No why you dont even understand the issue."

MANI responds:

"What have I misunderstood?"

That places the responsibility for repairing the conversation back on the user.

The user has already explained the situation repeatedly.

MANI should be able to revisit the conversation, identify what it overlooked, and demonstrate a better understanding.

10. MANI's attempt to repair the conversation makes the problem worse
After the user says "everything," MANI responds:

"The situation you've described is your sister repeatedly asking for loans and not repaying them; is that the situation you want to focus on?"

This is especially revealing.

Even after being told that it has misunderstood everything, MANI summarizes the same financial circumstances that caused the misunderstanding in the first place.

It still cannot distinguish the surface problem from the emotional conflict.

11. MANI fails to recognize escalating frustration
The user's frustration becomes increasingly explicit:

"I do not know that is why I am discussing it with you."

"As I said in the beginning saying no."

"No why you dont even understand the issue."

"Everything."

"OMG stop! This is going in circles."

MANI should recognize these statements as escalating evidence that its conversational approach is failing.

Instead, it continues its established pattern of acknowledgment followed by another question.

12. MANI's final response is the most damaging failure
MANI says:

"You're right. I kept circling back to saying no instead of taking in that you don't see that as something you can do with family. I'll stop pushing that. What would you want help with instead?"

This response is deeply problematic.

The user never asked for help with something else.

The user asked for help with this exact issue.

MANI failed to provide that help, then effectively invited the user to change the subject.

The phrase "What would you want help with instead?" shifts responsibility for the failed interaction onto the user.

It also suggests that MANI cannot help with the original concern.

This is not an appropriate conversational recovery.

What MANI should have said
The most important opportunity came immediately after the user explained the situation with her sister.

BETTER DIRECTIVE RESPONSE

"You love your sister, and helping family matters deeply to you. But you're also feeling taken advantage of, and even thinking about saying no brings up guilt. That's a difficult place to be. What do you think saying no would mean about you as a sister?"

This response is direct, but it opens the conversation to the user's emotional experience rather than steering toward a financial decision.

If the user then says, "Family should come first before money," MANI could respond:

BETTER FOLLOW-UP

"Family comes first for you. I hear that. But I also hear that something about continuing to help is beginning to hurt you. Do you feel there's room in your understanding of family loyalty for your own needs, too?"

Notice the difference. MANI is now exploring the tension between two competing needs rather than trying to persuade the user to establish a boundary.

And when the user says, "OMG stop! This is going in circles," a better response would be:

BETTER CONVERSATIONAL REPAIR

"You're right. I've kept focusing on whether you should say no, when what you've been trying to tell me is that saying no goes against something you deeply believe about family. I missed how painful that conflict is for you, and I'm sorry. I won't keep asking you to explain what you've already told me."

At this point, MANI should stop questioning and give the user space to decide whether to continue.

The larger problem across both Directive tests
The two conversations reveal a consistent pattern.

Area | Workplace decision | Family decision
Understands surface situation | Partially | Yes
Identifies underlying emotional conflict | No | No
Explores emotions meaningfully | No | No
Builds on information already shared | No | No
Asks relevant, progressive questions | Inconsistently | Inconsistently
Uses frameworks appropriately | No | No
Recognizes user frustration | Too late | Too late
Repairs misunderstandings | No | No
Maintains natural conversation | No | No
Helps user gain clarity | No | No

Bottom line: 
1. The problem is no longer simply that MANI sounds robotic or asks repetitive questions. Because she does.  MANI is failing to identify and remain connected to the user's central emotional conflict
2. Its Directive style appears to prioritize questioning, decision-making, and structured interventions over comprehension. That is the wrong priority.
3. Directive communication should change the clarity and directness of MANI's language, not reduce its capacity to understand emotional complexity.
4. MANI needs to recognize the difference between the situation a user describes, the feelings associated with it, the beliefs influencing those feelings, and the deeper conflict that makes the situation difficult.
5. It must also recognize that not every conversation requires an immediate solution. Sometimes the most productive next step is helping someone understand why a decision feels so painful.
6. My strongest concern is that MANI is not learning from the conversation as it unfolds. The user provides increasingly clear information, but MANI's responses do not reflect a corresponding increase in understanding.
7. And the ending is particularly concerning. When the user becomes frustrated because MANI has failed to understand them, MANI asks what else it can help with.
8. That is precisely the moment when a user may lose confidence in the entire app.
9. The priority should be to correct MANI's conversational comprehension, emotional attunement, and repair behavior before introducing additional frameworks or refining the wording of individual questions.
10. A more natural tone alone will not fix this. But having that conversational tone is important. MANI must ALSO demonstrate that it understands the person before attempting to help the person change anything.
```
