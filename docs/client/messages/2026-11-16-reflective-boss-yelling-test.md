## manasa's feedback

Muhammad,

Attached below is the transcript and my feedback from the first workplace conversation I tested with MANI in
Reflective.

Feedback:

Overall Problem: MANI spent too much time asking questions and exploring the same concerns before introducing ABCDE.
Although the language was more natural and the reflections were relevant, the conversation lacked progression and did
not provide enough practical help.

Reflective does not mean endless exploration. It means helping the user understand their experience through
thoughtful reflection and meaningful insight, without making the conversation feel repetitive or unnecessarily long.

MANI was more focused on exploring and completing the framework than recognizing when the user had enough
understanding and needed help moving forward.

MANI asked too many questions. Every response before ABCDE ended with another question. The questions were relevant
individually, but collectively created fatigue without enough meaningful progress.

MANI repeated information instead of building on it. It repeatedly returned to the user's declining confidence, fear
of judgment, and self-doubt without contributing enough new insight.

MANI did not recognize when it had enough information. The user had already explained what happened, what they
believed about themselves, and how it affected their behavior. MANI continued exploring until the user explicitly
said they felt they were going in circles.

MANI repeated the conversation inside ABCDE. Steps A, B, and C revisited information already established. The user
had to explain that these points had been covered and that they wanted to focus on rebuilding confidence.

MANI moved through the framework mechanically. It progressed from A through E with limited exploration of the user's
answers. The framework felt like a sequence of questions rather than a natural continuation of the conversation.

MANI did not sufficiently respond to the user's goal. The user repeatedly expressed wanting to rebuild confidence and
stop doubting themselves. MANI acknowledged this but continued focusing on understanding the problem rather than
helping the user move toward that goal.

MANI's final response did not resolve the user's concern. The user explicitly said they understood that their boss's
criticism did not define their abilities but still struggled to believe it. MANI offered a general perspective
without helping them identify how to apply it when the criticism happened again.

MANI's language has improved. The responses were generally clearer, more natural, and more consistent with
Reflective. It also carried relevant information into ABCDE. However, improvements in language have not yet
translated into a more effective conversation.

Overall Conclusion: Reflective is showing improvement in language and continuity, but the central issue that we keep
highlighting is still conversational judgment. MANI needs to recognize when enough information has been gathered,
offer insight without relying on another question, and adapt its framework to what the user has already shared. The
user should not have to repeatedly explain that they are ready to move forward.
