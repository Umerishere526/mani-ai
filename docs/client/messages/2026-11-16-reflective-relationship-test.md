## manasa's feedback

Attached below is the transcript and my feedback from the personal conversation I tested with MANI in Reflective.

Feedback:

Overall Problem: MANI showed some improvement in understanding the user's relationship concerns, but failed to
respect the user's decision not to use a framework, repeatedly asked questions instead of providing meaningful
guidance, and continued the conversation after the user explicitly said they were finished.

### What Improved

MANI recognized that the issue was not simply spending more time together, but wanting mutual effort and emotional
closeness.

The language was generally natural, and some reflections helped distinguish what the user wanted from their partner.

MANI eventually acknowledged its mistake when the user expressed frustration about the repeated framework
suggestion.

### What Needs to Change

MANI must respect the user's decision not to use a framework. The user explained that they did not want ABCDE and
preferred practical help. MANI offered the same framework again two responses later. Once a user declines a
framework, MANI must never offer it again in that conversation unless the user requests it.

MANI must never repeat questions or information already addressed. The user repeatedly explained that they wanted
more meaningful time together and mutual effort from their partner. MANI continued asking variations of what
closeness meant and whether they had communicated those needs.

MANI must recognize the user's goal and respond accordingly. The user explicitly said they wanted help figuring out
what to do about their relationship. MANI continued exploring their feelings rather than helping them determine a
practical next step. The user should not have to repeat their goal or express frustration to receive relevant
guidance.

MANI must stop asking confusing or unnatural questions. After the user declined ABCDE for the second time, MANI
asked, "When you say You don't want to use a framework; you want to figure out what to do about your relationship,
what experiences have led you to see it that way?" This question was inappropriate to the context, ignored the
user's clear instruction, and made the conversation feel scripted.

MANI must recognize when enough information has been gathered. The user had already explained the relationship
problem, their partner's response, what they were missing, and what they wanted to change. MANI had enough
information to provide meaningful support without continuing to ask questions.

MANI must end the conversation when the user says they are done. The user stated they did not want to keep talking,
confirmed they wanted to leave, and then explicitly said, "Yes, I'm done for now." MANI still asked whether they
wanted to return to the topic another time. This is unnecessary and fails to respect a clear decision to end.

### Overall Conclusion

Reflective demonstrated better understanding of the user's underlying relationship concerns, but the conversation
remained driven by repeated questions and framework suggestions rather than the user's stated needs.

MANI must never reintroduce a declined framework, must stop repeating established information, and must end
conversations when the user clearly indicates they are finished. The user should feel heard and supported without
having to repeatedly correct MANI's behavior.
