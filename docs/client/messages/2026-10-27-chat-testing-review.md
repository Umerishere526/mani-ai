## What it is

Six test conversations, two situations in all three styles: a personal one (weight loss, motivation, no time for
herself) and a workplace one (a preschool co-teacher who does less of the work and sometimes falls asleep or is on her
phone while the children are there). The tester felt heard in every one and helped in few: replies summarised,
validated and asked again, the three styles moved the same way, a direct ask for help got another question, ABCDE and
Thought Reframe were used on something really happening and on someone who already saw both sides, the child-safety
risk was not named, and the body check-in started without asking.

("Mani understands, then helps", branch `feat/understand-then-help`). They were pasted in that session and are not

MANI Chat Testing Review

Personal or Emotionally Challenging Situation
Background and Testing Approach
For the personal challenge, I approached MANI as someone struggling with weight loss,
motivation and maintaining healthier eating and exercise habits.

1. Direct
What Worked Well
I appreciated that MANI followed the conversation closely and demonstrated a strong
understanding of the challenges I was sharing. It approached my concerns without judgment and
recognized that my difficulties weren't simply about motivation or willpower, but also involved
practical and emotional factors. There were moments when MANI made meaningful connections
between my daily responsibilities, limited time for myself, and frustration with not being able to
make the changes I wanted. I appreciated that it recognized the larger picture rather than
focusing exclusively on weight loss.

What Could Be Improved
My biggest concern was that the conversation lacked direction and progression. Although MANI
consistently acknowledged what I shared, it continued asking questions without moving toward
anything particularly helpful. Many responses followed the same pattern: MANI would
summarize what I had said, validate the experience, and then ask another question. As the
conversation progressed, the questions became increasingly repetitive, often revisiting challenges
we had already explored. I found myself wondering what the purpose of the conversation was and
what we were actually working toward. I kept waiting for something to happen, whether that was
practical advice, guidance, an exercise, a different perspective, or a meaningful insight. Instead, I
felt like I was answering question after question without gaining anything new from the
interaction.

How the Direct Style Felt
Given that this was the Direct communication style, I expected a more straightforward and
solution-oriented approach. Instead, the conversation felt more reflective than directive. Toward
the end, I directly mentioned that we were going in circles. MANI acknowledged the repetition
and apologized, which I appreciated. However, by that point, the interaction had already become
frustrating, and the conversation ended without any meaningful guidance.

Recommendations

I would recommend making the Direct style more distinct by incorporating practical guidance
and actionable suggestions earlier in the conversation. Once MANI has gathered enough
information to understand the user's concerns, it could help identify realistic and manageable
next steps rather than continuing to explore the same challenges. I would also encourage MANI
to recognize when a conversation is becoming repetitive and adjust its approach without
requiring the user to point it out. This could involve offering a relevant exercise, introducing a
different perspective, or checking whether the user would prefer practical guidance

or further exploration.

Conclusion
Although MANI was attentive, understanding, and emotionally validating, I didn't feel that the
Direct style delivered the experience I expected. I felt heard, but I didn't necessarily feel helped. I
would have appreciated a greater sense of purpose and something useful to take away from the
conversation.

2. Supportive
What Worked Well
I appreciated MANI's gentle, compassionate, and nonjudgmental approach throughout the
conversation. It acknowledged the challenges I was experiencing and recognized that my
difficulties involved both emotional and practical barriers rather than simply a lack of willpower.
One moment I particularly appreciated was when I responded, "I don't know, I'm sorry," and

appropriate, supportive, and emotionally attuned.

What Could Be Improved
Although MANI consistently offered validation, the conversation became repetitive and overly
focused on asking questions. Several questions felt like variations of things I had already
answered, particularly around exhaustion, guilt, and making time for myself. There were also
moments when I expressed that multiple things felt equally difficult, but MANI continued asking
me to identify which was most challenging. This made the conversation feel somewhat rigid
rather than responsive to what I was communicating. I also noticed that MANI occasionally
interpreted my experience more deeply than I had necessarily expressed. For example, it
suggested that I might feel I didn't deserve to prioritize my own needs. While this could be worth
exploring, I would have preferred a more open-ended approach rather than introducing that
interpretation through a forced choice. Toward the end, when I mentioned that we had already
discussed something, MANI acknowledged the repetition and adjusted its response. I appreciated
that it was receptive to my feedback, although I would have liked it to recognize the pattern
earlier.

How the Supportive Style Felt
The Supportive style felt warm, compassionate, and emotionally validating. However, as the
conversation progressed, I found myself becoming increasingly frustrated because it felt like we
were going in circles. I kept waiting for something more to happen, whether that was guidance,
encouragement, an exercise, or a different perspective. Instead, the conversation continued with
question after question. I felt heard and understood, but I didn't necessarily feel helped. By the
end, I was left wondering what I had actually gained from the interaction. While the tone was
supportive, the experience lacked meaningful progression.

Recommendations

I would recommend that MANI balance emotional validation with a greater sense of direction.

While reassurance and reflective listening are important aspects of a supportive conversation,

repeatedly asking them to prioritize concerns they have already identified as equally
important. Additionally, when a user directly asks for an exercise or suggestion, MANI could
offer a simple, accessible option rather than immediately responding with another question,

Conclusion

style in terms of conversational structure. Both relied heavily on reflecting back what I shared
and asking another question. I think the experience would have been more helpful if MANI
occasionally paused to check whether I wanted to continue exploring my feelings or move
toward something more practical. Support doesn't necessarily require providing solutions, but I
would have appreciated leaving the conversation with a new perspective, greater clarity, or
something meaningful to take away.

3. Reflective

I was sharing. It recognized the connection between wanting to make healthier changes, feeling
discouraged when I couldn't maintain them, and the larger demands of my everyday

Similar to the Direct and Supportive conversations, I found that the Reflective style became

Recommendations
I would recommend that MANI focus on creating more meaningful reflection rather than relying

advice or solutions, but they should help users develop greater awareness, recognize patterns, or

questions that encourage self-discovery. This might involve gently highlighting a recurring
pattern, drawing attention to a meaningful connection, or checking whether the current
exploration is still addressing the user's needs.

Conclusion
Of the three styles, Reflective was the one where I most expected ongoing emotional exploration.

gain greater clarity. I think a stronger Reflective experience would move beyond accurately

Instead, the conversation focused primarily on identifying, questioning, and reframing thoughts.

Recommendations
I would suggest allowing more flexibility in how MANI selects therapeutic interventions. Rather
than automatically moving toward cognitive restructuring, MANI could first assess whether the
user is describing an internal belief, an external circumstance, or a combination of both. For the
Direct style, I would prioritize concrete guidance and actionable steps while still maintaining

more open-ended questions, and making the transition into exercises more collaborative.
