## The chat it reviews

A Directive conversation on the chat tester: a person who feels they are the only one making an effort in their
friendships, five messages of chat, ABCDE offered and tried, and a new belief at E ("Maybe I don't have to keep proving
that I'm worth remembering"). Two of the person's messages appear twice, word for word, at B to C and at C to D.

## The review (summary of its points, in its words where quoted)

- Overall: "a strong chat and framework conversation". MANI understood the central issue, explored the emotional
  impact, and helped the user reach a more balanced view "without dismissing the fact that their friendships may
  genuinely have become less reciprocal". "I wouldn't redesign this cadence. The overall structure is working."
- Problem, desire, need and feelings: captured well. The desire's key sentence: "I want to feel wanted without having
  to remind people that I'm there."
- Underlying feelings: good, could deepen. Fear of rejection, insecurity and shame are reasonable readings, but "they
  should remain interpretations rather than being treated as confirmed emotional facts."
- Before the framework: give a significant emotional moment another turn, for example: "It sounds like there's a worry
  that if you stopped making the effort, you might discover these friendships don't hold the same place in your
  friends' lives as they do in yours. What is it like carrying that uncertainty?" Not in every conversation.
- The move to the framework could be more conversational: "It's difficult because continuing to make the effort leaves
  you feeling hurt, but stepping back feels like you might lose people who still mean a lot to you. There's a framework
  called ABCDE that could help us look at the belief behind that and whether there's another way to understand your
  place in these friendships. Would you like to try it?"
- Repeated answers: the person repeated their previous answer twice; MANI "should not treat repeated content as new
  information or automatically move forward as though the question was answered." At D it could say: "I can see how
  those experiences have affected you. And I don't want to overlook the times your friends have shown up for you
  either. How do those two sides sit together for you?"
- After E: "a gentle question about what the user wants to do with this new perspective", such as "How would you like
  to approach these friendships differently, without feeling that you're putting them at risk?", without making every
  framework end with an action plan. The closing reflection was "a little conclusive".
- The four changes it asks for: give emotional moments more room; recognise repeated answers; use chat context more
  naturally in the framework, never asking what was already answered; connect the new belief to the person's life.
  "The next improvement is making the conversation feel even more emotionally complete, not adding more questions or
  more framework stages."
