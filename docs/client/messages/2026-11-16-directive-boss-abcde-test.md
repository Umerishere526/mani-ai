## Lolly's review

Testing DIRECTIVE

Overall Problem: MANI moved too quickly from one question to the next and rushed through the ABCDE framework without
taking enough time to understand the user's experience.

It needed to slow down, listen more carefully, explore the user's responses in greater depth, and meet the user where
they were emotionally.

Directive does not mean rushing. It means being clear, focused, and helpful while allowing enough conversation to
understand the problem before moving toward solutions.

MANI was moving through steps instead of having a meaningful conversation.

1. MANI asked too many questions. Instead of helping the user address the problem, it kept asking for more
   information.

2. MANI did not listen carefully. The user clearly explained that the boss repeatedly yelled at and disrespected them.
   MANI kept asking for more details.

3. MANI repeated itself. It kept restating what the user had already said without adding anything useful.

4. MANI made assumptions. It suggested what the user might be feeling instead of allowing the user to explain.

5. MANI did not follow the Directive style. The conversation was too slow, repetitive, and focused on feelings rather
   than practical help.

6  MANI apologized repeatedly but did not change its behavior. It acknowledged mistakes and then continued making
   similar mistakes.

7. MANI introduced ABCDE without enough understanding. It moved into an exercise instead of first establishing what
   the user needed.

8. MANI repeated questions inside ABCDE.  QUESTION for the team do we like the way mani handles ABC in the framework?

9. MANI rushed through the framework. It moved from A to B to C to D without properly exploring the user's answers.

10. MANI ignored important information. When the user said there were more feelings and consequences, MANI did not
    ask about them.

11. MANI moved to Step D too quickly. It started challenging the user's belief before fully understanding the impact
    of the situation.

12. MANI followed the framework instead of the conversation. It appeared more focused on completing the steps than
    responding to what the user was saying.

13. MANI lost the user's trust. The user became frustrated, felt unheard, and ended the conversation.
