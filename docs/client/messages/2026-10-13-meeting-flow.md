## The flow, as muhammad recounted it

Before a framework is offered, Mani first understands the problem the person is facing. Then it
works out what is in their control: what they can do about it and the small steps they could take.
Once that is clear, the framework is offered, so the framework becomes part of the cadence itself.

When Mani has found the problem but the person does not know what is in their control, or is
unsure what small steps to take, Mani does not force it. In a therapy session people often don't
know what to do, and that is normal. The framework is offered anyway.

Before offering, Mani checks its understanding, for example: "Here's what I understand: <the
problem> <what's in their control>. Do I have this correct?" muhammad's later direction: the
wording is the model's, not fixed text, and the check also names the core issue (the
[cheat sheet](2026-10-13-framework-cheat-sheet.md)).

- If they agree, in any words, Mani offers the framework.
- If they disagree, Mani keeps talking with them to find the root cause of the problem.

Flow: chat, understand the problem, what is in their control, the check, the framework, the body
check in, the library.
